# Based on this list, find me good example/illustration, that might be relevant for some chapters of my book. (don't hesitate to go through blog articles or welcome to the jungle page/job description to understand the values and culture). As a reminder, structure of my book with its structure:

Part 1 — Understand Your Ecosystem
Read the board before you move a piece.
Chapter 1: Mapping the Terrain (Market Insights)
Keywords: Profit pools, Legal/Regulatory Catalysts, TAM/SAM/SOM, Market Maturity
Stop guessing where the money is. This chapter teaches you to map profit pools and quantify the market size (TAM/SAM/SOM) by geography and industry. We move beyond basic sizing to identify Catalysts: the specific shifts (shortage of professionals, tech enablers, compliance directives like data residency or health reforms) that create urgency. You will learn to identify where demand meets margin and assess market maturity to ensure you aren't too early or too late.
Chapter 2: The Enemy is Inaction (Competitive Intelligence)
Keywords: Status quo bias, Teardown Analysis, Indirect Alternatives, Win/Loss
Your biggest competitor isn’t X or Y; it’s the customer deciding to stick with spreadsheets, pen \& paper, or "internship" workarounds. We move beyond feature checklists to perform a Teardown Analysis: identifying the competition's winning features, their specific gaps, and their pricing/packaging weaknesses. You will learn to build battlecards that actually help Sales close by focusing on the unique narrative that exposes where others fail to meet expectations.
Chapter 3: Who Actually Signs the Check? (Buyer \& Customer Knowledge)
Keywords: Buying committee, Emotional Drivers, Triggers, Jobs to be Done
Users might love you, but buyers pay you. We map the entire purchasing committee and deeply segment them by cluster fit (maturity, specific needs). Crucially, we distinguish between Functional Aspirations (simplicity, reliability) and Emotional Drivers (trust, fairness, peace of mind). We will identify the specific Triggers \& Buying Signals—what actually starts the journey?—and map the current frictions (setup time, complexity overload) that prevent the sale.
Part 2 — Link Your Product and Your Business
Tie opportunities to margin and payback.
Chapter 4: The Economic Model (Business Strategy \& P\&L)
Keywords: Magic Ratio, Adoption Assumptions, New MRR, Gross Margin
Product leaders must speak Finance. Here, we connect the roadmap to the P\&L. You will learn to forecast the ROI using a High-Level Financial Model that tracks New MRR, Expansion, and the "Magic Ratio." We will explicitly document your Assumptions (adoption rate, ramping %, average revenue per account) so that you aren't just betting on features, but betting on measurable financial movement.
Chapter 5: Pricing and Packaging as Product
Keywords: Good-Better-Best, Pricing Guardrails, Offer Architecture, Bundling
Pricing is a psychological design challenge. We treat pricing as a feature. You will learn to draft a Good-Better-Best architecture, defining what goes into Add-ons, Bundles, or Base Seats. We will establish Pricing Guardrails (floors, maximum discounts) and design Pricing Experiments to test your hypotheses. The goal is to create an Offer Architecture that forces an upsell logic (e.g., usage-based vs. flat fee) directly into the product.
Chapter 6: Designing Distribution (PLG, SLG, \& Hybrid)
Keywords: Foot-in-the-door, Partnership Strategy, Commissioning, Funnels
Distribution is a design constraint.
For SLG: We define the Strategic Lever (e.g., foot-in-the-door vs. anchoring high).
For PLG: We design the Engagement Plan—freemium duration, credit-based trials, and conversion triggers.
For Partnerships: We build a strategy for Channel Distribution and Big Logos, including how commissioning works to incentivize external sellers.
Chapter 7: The Revenue-First Roadmap (Shape Your Product)
Keywords: Anti-Scope, Gold-Plating, Feature-to-Benefit, ROI
Move from "feature factories" to "investment portfolios." This chapter focuses on Product Knowledge: defining not just what we build, but What our product DOESN’T solve. We will ruthlessly cut "Gold-plating" and edge cases that don't drive revenue. You will learn to craft the Product Pitch (Elevator pitch, Feature-to-Benefit translation) and align the roadmap to three expected results: \#BestCare (Quality), \#HappierWorkLife (Efficiency), and \#GrowthRevenue (Impact).
Chapter 8: The Narrative That Sells (Positioning \& Vision)
Keywords: Category Definition, Value Pillars, Unfair Advantage
Features tell; value sells. We will define your Product Category and your Strategic Differentiator (Unfair Advantage). You will synthesize your Value Pillars—the core truths that underpin your differentiation. This ensures that when you explain your product, you aren't listing features, you are defining the standard for your category.
Part 3 — Put It in Customers’ Hands
Orchestrate the landing, not just the launch.
Chapter 9: GTM Readiness (Prepare)
Keywords: Narrative Snacks, Objection Handling, Sales vs. AM, Cockpit
Amateurs practice until they get it right; professionals practice until they can’t get it wrong. We build the "Enablement Plan": defining who sells (Hunter) vs. who upsells (Farmer/AM). We create "Narrative Snacks" (structured short-form messaging) for internal consumption and detailed Objection Handling scripts. This is about Revenue Enablement: ensuring the field is armed with battlecards, scripts, and confidence.
Chapter 10: Orchestrating the Launch (Execute)
Keywords: Work-back Timeline, Main Checklist, Billing Readiness, Phasing
A launch is a coordinated military maneuver. We build the Main Checklist covering Strategy, Product Readiness (MVP, Performance, Billing/Farming capabilities), and Content. We utilize a Work-back Timeline to ensure legal, ops, and product land simultaneously. We verify Billing Readiness—can we actually invoice for this?—before we turn on the lights.
Chapter 11: The Demand Engine (Promote \& Awareness)
Keywords: USP, Content Plan, Growth Loops, Attribution
Turn the launch spike into a permanent plateau of growth. We refine the Unique Selling Proposition (USP) and execute an Exhaustive Content Plan. This chapter connects the "Launch" to the "Life" of the product, ensuring that marketing channels (Paid, PR, Partners) continue to feed the product funnel with qualified intent long after the initial announcement.
Part 4 — Portfolio Performance
Operate like an investor.
Chapter 12: Activation and Expansion (Activate)
Keywords: Time-to-value, Expansion/Upsell Triggers, Retention
Acquisition is vanity; retention is sanity. We focus on Activation: fixing the "leaky bucket" of early churn. Then we pivot to Expansion: using the data triggers identified in Part 1 to drive Account Management motions—moving customers from "Good" to "Better" to "Best" plans systematically.
Chapter 13: The Feedback Loop (Iterate)
Keywords: Evidence-based decisions, Continuous improvement, Verbatims
Close the loop or die. We establish systems to ingest unstructured data and Verbatims. We prioritize inputs based on the financial model from Chapter 4—fixing the issues that block revenue, not just the ones that are "loud."
Chapter 14: The Operating System (Financial Performance \& Rhythm)
Keywords: Portfolio reviews, Churn Baseline, Ramping %, Rule of 40
This is the capstone. How do you manage all of this? We implement the Operating System: tracking the Expected Results defined in Chapter 7 against the Financial Model defined in Chapter 4. You will learn to monitor Churn Baselines, Ramping percentages, and NRR to manage your product lines like a financial portfolio.
