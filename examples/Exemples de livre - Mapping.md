## PART 1: UNDERSTAND YOUR ECOSYSTEM

### **Chapter 1: Mapping the Terrain (Market Insights)**
*Keywords: Profit pools, Legal/Regulatory Catalysts, TAM/SAM/SOM, Market Maturity*

#### **Primary Example: Qonto (B2B Fintech – SME Banking)**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- **Profit Pool Identification**: Qonto identified that European SMEs were massively underserved by banks. Over 21 million SMEs in its target markets; traditional banks ignored the <€10K monthly spend segment as "too small to matter." Qonto realized the profit pool wasn't in mega-clients but in **volume + retention**.
- **Legal/Regulatory Catalyst**: French banking regulation changes (PSD2, open banking requirements) created the **exact window of opportunity**. Legacy banks couldn't move fast enough to comply; Qonto built natively with those constraints.
- **TAM/SAM/SOM Precision**:
  - TAM: €52 billion (total SME finance market across 8 European countries)
  - SAM: €15-20 billion (addressable by fintech, excluding legacy banking entrenched)
  - SOM (Year 1-3): €500M-€1B (Qonto's realistic capture at scale)
- **Market Maturity**: 2015-2018 = early (pre-mobile-first SME banking); 2018-2022 = growth; 2022+ = competition entering.

**What to teach from Qonto:**
- How to size TAM without generic "consulting deck" language
- How regulatory shifts (GDPR, PSD2, open banking APIs) create urgency AND barriers to entry
- How to pick a "wedge" (SME current accounts) that opens the door to expansion (invoicing → spend management → embedded finance)

**Data you can cite:**
- "Qonto now serves 600K+ customers, but that's only 3% of its addressable SME market across 8 countries."
- "Monthly friction: a typical SME loses 5-8 hours per month on accounting/payment reconciliation (the 'jobs to be done' catalyst)."

### **Chapter 2: The Enemy is Inaction (Competitive Intelligence)**

*Keywords: Status quo bias, Teardown Analysis, Indirect Alternatives, Win/Loss*

#### **Primary Example: Qonto vs. Status Quo (SMEs' Current Reality)**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- **Direct Competitors**: Revolut (trying to be "everything bank"), Wise (money transfer only), Traditional Banks (Deutsche Bank's "Vert", BNP's digital offerings)
- **Indirect Competitors (Status Quo Bias)**: Excel + accountant, QuickBooks + manual bank imports, pen-and-paper reconciliation
- **Qonto's Key Insight**: The biggest competitor isn't a bank; it's inertia. An SME founder loses 5-8 hours/month on accounting but hasn't switched because of switching cost, fear of data loss, and "good enough" status quo.

**Teardown Analysis (what Qonto learned):**

| Dimension | Excel + Accountant | Traditional Bank | Revolut | **Qonto** |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|----------|
| Setup Time | Weeks (manual) | 2-3 weeks (slow onboarding) | 5 minutes | 3 minutes |
| Integration | None (manual) | Limited APIs | Decent | **Native invoicing + integrations** |
| Real-time Spend Visibility | No (monthly reconciliation) | No (1-3 day lag) | Basic | **Real-time dashboard** |
| Compliance/Tax Features | Manual (error-prone) | Designed for retail | None | **Built-in for SME reporting** |
| Pricing | €100-200/month (accountant) | €50-100/month | €0-15/month | €9-49/month |
| **Emotional Driver** | "I don't want to learn tech" | "My bank is fine, I've been with them for 20 years" | "I don't trust fintech for business" | **"Finally, built for me"** |

**What to teach from this:**
- How to map **both direct and indirect competitors** (spreadsheets are a bigger competitor than Revolut)
- How to identify the **friction points** where competitors fail (accountants are great for tax, terrible for real-time visibility)
- How to construct a **"jobs to be done"** narrative that positions your product against the status quo's emotional barriers

**Qonto's Win/Loss narrative:**
- **Why they win**: Real-time visibility + invoicing + compliance, all in one place
- **Why they lose** (to status quo): "I'm worried about data security in the cloud" → Qonto's answer: "We're licensed, encrypted, GDPR-compliant"
- **Why they lose** (to competitors): Revolut's brand + minimal setup time for basic users

### **Chapter 4: The Economic Model (Business Strategy & P&L)**

*Keywords: Magic Ratio, Adoption Assumptions, New MRR, Gross Margin*

#### **Primary Example: Qonto's Financial Model & Magic Ratio**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- Qonto is fundamentally a **unit economics story**
- Starting assumptions (that matter for the P&L):

| Assumption | Qonto's Reality | Impact on P&L |
|---|---|---|
| **Adoption Rate (SME Penetration)** | Target: 2-3% of 21M SMEs in Europe (Phase 1) = 420K-630K customers by Year 5 | **New MRR** generation: +€10-15M annually if adoption hits targets |
| **ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)** | €15-25/month (wide range: solo freelancers vs. 50-person firms) | **Gross Margin**: depends on payment processing costs (2-3% of revenue) |
| **Expansion Revenue** | SMEs graduate from €15/month to €35/month as they add invoicing + spend management | **NRR target**: 110%+ (retention + expansion) |
| **Churn Rate** | Target: <2% monthly (very low because switching cost is high; SMEs don't want to re-enter accounting data) | **Lifetime Value** = €15/month × 60 months × 98% retention = €900+ per customer |

**Qonto's "Magic Ratio"** (SaaS rule of thumb: Magic Ratio = NRR / CAC Payback):
- **New MRR**: €2M/month (cohort of new customers added)
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**: ~€50-100 per customer (mostly content + SME networks)
- **CAC Payback**: 2-4 months (very efficient for B2B SaaS)
- **Magic Ratio**: (110% NRR) / (3-month payback) = **highly efficient** (benchmark: >0.75 is strong)

**What to teach from this:**
- How to estimate **adoption rate** (what % of TAM will actually switch?)
- How to model **expansion revenue** (how much does average customer spend over time?)
- How to connect product roadmap to **P&L levers**: "Adding invoicing feature should increase ARPU by €8-10/month and reduce churn by 0.5%"

**Assumption you can test:**
- "If we improve onboarding (reduce setup time from 30 min to 10 min), adoption rate improves 10%."
- That 10% improvement adds ___ to NRR.

#### **Secondary Example: Back Market's Gross Margin by Channel**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- Back Market has **two revenue streams** with different unit economics:
  - **B2C** (individual refurbished electronics buyers): 15-20% gross margin (after seller payouts, quality control, fraud)

- Product implications: Build features that attract B2B deals (bulk purchasing, contract terms, IT asset management) → improve overall gross margin profile

### **Chapter 5: Pricing and Packaging as Product**

*Keywords: Good-Better-Best, Pricing Guardrails, Offer Architecture, Bundling*

#### **Primary Example: Alan's Subscription + Tier Architecture**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- Alan uses a **hybrid model**:
  - **Per-member subscription**: €1-3/month per employee (the "base")
  - **Services add-ons**: Mental health, prevention programs, care navigation (upsells)

**Good-Better-Best Architecture (Implicit in Alan's Model):**

| Tier | What's Included | Monthly Cost (per employee) | When a Company Buys It |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Good (Core)** | Basic health insurance coverage | €1.50 | "I just need minimum coverage" |
| **Better (Base + Care Navigation)** | Core + AI-driven health navigation, preventive care | €2.20 | "We want to reduce healthcare costs and improve health outcomes" |
| **Best (Full Super-App)** | All above + mental health, wellness programs, telemedicine | €3.00+ | "We're competing for talent; benefits = culture" |

**Why this pricing architecture works:**
1. **Psychological**: "€1.50/month" sounds affordable; anchoring effect makes "€3/month" seem reasonable
2. **Churn reduction**: Each upsell creates a new "switching cost" (team now depends on mental health integration)
3. **Expansion**: Alan can measure which companies adopt which tier and build product roadmap accordingly (e.g., if 60% adopt mental health, build that more aggressively)

**Pricing Guardrails Alan Likely Enforces:**
- Minimum discount: -20% (can't go below)
- Maximum discount: -40% (for 3-year contracts)
- Premium multiplier: +50% for white-label / white-glove service

**What to teach from this:**
- How to move from "one price fits all" to a **tiered system** that captures willingness-to-pay
- How to use **add-ons** (mental health, prevention) as expansion levers, not just features
- How to set pricing guardrails so Sales doesn't blow through margin

#### **Secondary Example: Qonto's Pricing Expansion Over Time**

**Qonto's Pricing Journey:**
- **Year 1**: €9/month (solo), €29/month (team) — simple, wins on affordability
- **Year 2-3**: Added invoice management (+€5-10/month), spend management add-on (+€10-15/month)

**Why this matters:**

### **Chapter 6: Designing Distribution (PLG, SLG, & Hybrid)**

*Keywords: Foot-in-the-door, Partnership Strategy, Commissioning, Funnels*

#### **Primary Example: Miro (PLG Strategy)**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- Miro is the **canonical PLG example** for European SaaS
- Core distribution strategy: "Freemium with unlimited users"

**Miro's PLG Distribution Architecture:**

| Tactic | How It Works | Revenue Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Free tier with unlimited users** | Any user can create a free account; they can add unlimited collaborators on that account (unusual for SaaS) | Creates viral loop: 1 user invites 5, all are users, only 1 converts to paid |
| **1,000+ templates** | Pre-built templates for retrospectives, user research, roadmapping, etc. | Reduces "time to first value" from days to minutes; instant productivity |
| **Personalization onboarding** | 7-question survey → templates recommended based on role (designer, PM, developer) | Improves activation rate; different user sees different value prop |
| **Tight integration with tools** | Slack, Jira, Google Workspace integrations (100+ total) | Makes Miro "sticky" by becoming the hub for collaborative workflows |
| **Community + Content** | Miroverse community, webinars, templates marketplace | Organic growth; users generate templates; low CAC |

**Miro's Funnel Metrics** (from case studies):
- **Viral loops**: 500% user growth (5M to 30M users, 2020-2022)
- **Free-to-paid conversion**: ~10-15% of free users convert to paid (high for PLG)
- **MoM Growth**: Consistently 15-20% MoM without large marketing spend
- **CAC**: Near-zero for viral users; ~$20-30 for paid ads (very efficient)

**Distribution Levers Miro Uses:**
1. **Foot-in-the-door**: Free account requires no credit card; lowest friction
2. **Viral mechanics**: "Invite 5 teammates" is baked into product experience
3. **Partner ecosystem**: 100+ integrations = becomes indispensable in tech stack

**What to teach from this:**
- How to design a **freemium model** that creates viral loops (unlimited users on free = team needs to get everyone on same plan)
- How to use **templates** as distribution (templates = reason to sign up; templates = network effect)
- How to measure **PLG health**: not just conversion rate, but MoM growth, time to first value, NRR

### **Chapter 7: The Revenue-First Roadmap (Shape Your Product)**

*Keywords: Anti-Scope, Gold-Plating, Feature-to-Benefit, ROI*

#### **Primary Example: Qonto's Roadmap Discipline**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- Qonto started with **one sharp problem**: "SME needs a better current account"
- They explicitly **didn't build** a dozen features upfront
- Their roadmap evolution:

| Phase | What They Built | What They Deliberately Didn't Build | Revenue Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 (v0)** | Business current account, card, online access | Lending, insurance, accounting integrations | MVP: €9/month, high adoption (50K users) |
| **Phase 2 (v1)** | + Invoicing module | Lending, accounting integrations, expense categorization | +€5-10 ARPU from invoicing add-on |
| **Phase 3 (v2)** | + Spend management, receipt capture | Lending (not ready), insurance | +€10 ARPU; also improves GRR (stickiness) |
| **Phase 4 (v3)** | + Embedded financing (via partnerships) | Building lending themselves (too capital-intensive) | Launches financing products; +5-8% NRR from upsell |

**Anti-Scope Decisions (What Qonto Said No To):**
- **Insurance**: Too capital-intensive, not core to their wedge
- **Accounting software**: Better to integrate QuickBooks/Xero than rebuild
- **Investment products**: Outside their regulatory expertise as SME bank

**What to teach from this:**
- How to define **"what we don't do"** as sharply as what we do
- How to measure roadmap items by **revenue impact**, not feature requests
- How to sequence features by **adoption curve**: invoicing (high demand) before financing (lower demand, higher complexity)

**Question you can ask leaders:**
- "If you cut this feature from the roadmap, how much revenue would you lose in Year 1?" (If answer is <€500K, maybe don't build it)

| T-12 months | T-6 months | T-3 months | T-1 month | T-0 (Launch) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finalize retail concept; secure first store location | Build retail POS system; train staff | Hire store managers; integrate with inventory system; test billing | Pre-marketing campaign; final ops readiness | Grand opening |

**Main Checklist for Retail Launch:**

**Strategy ✓**
- [ ] Define store format (refurbished phones, laptops, all categories?)
- [ ] Define store economics (target COGS, margin, traffic assumptions)
- [ ] Legal: Liability waivers, warranty terms for in-store purchases

**Product Readiness ✓**
- [ ] POS system integration (in-store inventory system syncs with online)

**Billing Readiness ✓**
- [ ] Payment processor integration (can we take card, Apple Pay, Alipay?)

**Content ✓**
- [ ] In-store signage: "100% refurbished. 1-year warranty. Certified quality."

- **Week 1-2**: London flagship store (learn, iterate)

**What to teach from this:**
- How to build a **work-back timeline** that connects all teams (product, ops, legal, finance can't launch independently)
- How to do a **billing readiness check** before launch (can you actually invoice for this?)
- How to phase a launch (one flagship to learn, then scale)

### **Chapter 13: The Feedback Loop (Iterate)**

*Keywords: Evidence-based decisions, Continuous improvement, Verbatims*

#### **Primary Example: Qonto's Iteration Cycle**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- Qonto uses **customer feedback loops** to prioritize roadmap
- Each feature should be justified by either:
  1. Customer interviews + verbatims
  2. Product data (usage, churn correlation)
  3. Financial impact (estimated revenue)

**Qonto's Feedback Mechanisms:**

| Feedback Source | What They Learn | How They Prioritize |
|---|---|---|
| **Support tickets** | "Customers ask how to reconcile automatically 50x/month" | High signal: invoicing + reconciliation feature ranked #1 |
| **Churn surveys** | "We left because we still needed to export to accountant" | High signal: accounting software integrations prioritized |
| **Feature requests (in-app)** | "Customers vote: 200 want APIs, 50 want mobile app" | Medium signal: 200 votes = maybe not, could be vocal minority |
| **Usage data (product)** | "Only 30% of users view the spend dashboard" | Medium signal: maybe dashboard UX is confusing; redesign it |
| **Win/Loss (Sales)** | "We lose to Revolut because setup is 5 min vs. our 3 min" | Low signal: Revolut is actually slower, this is outdated objection |
| **NRR data** | "Customers who adopt invoicing have 15% higher NRR" | High signal: Invoicing isn't just a feature; it's a retention lever |

**Prioritization Framework Qonto Likely Uses:**

```
Priority Score = (Customer Requests / Total Requests) × (Revenue Impact / Total Opportunities) × (Churn Reduction / 100)

Example 1: Invoicing
= (200 requests / 1,000) × (€3-5 per user / €20 total TAM per user) × (15% churn reduction)
= 0.20 × 0.20 × 0.15 = 0.006 (HIGH PRIORITY)

Example 2: Mobile App
= (50 requests / 1,000) × (€0-1 per user / €20 total TAM) × (2% churn reduction)
= 0.05 × 0.05 × 0.02 = 0.00005 (LOW PRIORITY)
```

**Verbatims Qonto Collects:**

From support/interviews:
- "I love Qonto's simplicity, but I still have to manually track my invoices in Excel" → Feature: Invoicing integration
- "I'm worried about switching because my accountant only knows my old bank" → Feature: Accountant integration, dashboard
- "I don't know if my business is profitable; Qonto shows me only today's cash balance" → Feature: Profitability dashboard

**What to teach from this:**
- How to **weight feedback sources**: Support tickets + churn surveys (high signal) vs. feature requests (medium signal) vs. sales objections (often outdated)
- How to extract **verbatims** that reveal the real jobs to be done (not the feature request, but the underlying need)
- How to **quantify** the impact of feedback on revenue/churn before building

### **Chapter 14: The Operating System (Financial Performance & Rhythm)**

*Keywords: Portfolio reviews, Churn Baseline, Ramping %, Rule of 40*

#### **Primary Example: European SaaS Operating Rhythm (Composite)**

**Why it's perfect for this chapter:**
- This chapter brings together **financial discipline + product strategy**
- How to manage product portfolio like an investor

**Monthly Operating Rhythm (What Leaders Review):**

| Meeting | Cadence | Key Metrics | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exec Dashboard Review** | Weekly | MRR, ARR, churn, NRR, CAC payback | "Are we on track to hit quarterly target?" |
| **Product Portfolio Review** | Monthly | New MRR by feature, feature adoption, time-to-value, retention by cohort | "Which features are we killing? Which are we investing in?" |
| **Churn Postmortem** | Monthly | Churn rate by segment, reason for churn (NPS verbatims) | "Why did we lose ABC Corp? What do we fix?" |
| **Financial Forecast Update** | Monthly | Projection of Year-end ARR, runway, Rule of 40 score | "Do we need to fundraise? Are we on pace for profitability?" |
| **Expansion/NRR Review** | Quarterly | NRR by cohort, expansion revenue by product line, expansion triggers firing? | "Is upsell working? Do we need product changes?" |

**Key Financial Metrics Leaders Track:**

**Churn Baseline:**
- Qonto might target: <2% monthly churn
- Alan might target: <5% annual churn (health insurance is sticky)
- If actual > target: **Root cause analysis** (UX problem? Pricing? Competitor win?)

**Ramping %:**
- New customer's revenue ramp (% of expected ARPU after 6 months)
- If ramping % is low: "Are we onboarding poorly? Is the product not sticky?" → Fix onboarding

**Rule of 40 Score:**
- Growth Rate (%) + FCF Margin (%) ≥ 40%
- For Qonto (hypothetical): 50% growth + 10% FCF margin = 60 score (good)
- If score drops: "Are we burning cash on GTM? Do we need to cut costs or slow growth?"

**What to teach from this:**
- How to conduct a **monthly product portfolio review** that ties product decisions to financial outcomes
- How to track **churn by reason** and tie it to product fixes (not just "churn is 3%"; know *why*)
- How to use **Rule of 40** as a health check: Are we growing AND being profitable?

**Example Cohort Analysis (Alan):**

| Cohort | Size | Churn | NRR | Revenue per Employee | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2023 Cohort | 50 companies | 8% YoY | 105% | €45K | "Stable; at baseline" |
| Q2 2023 Cohort | 60 companies | 12% YoY | 102% | €38K | "Higher churn; onboarding was weak" → Fix Q2 onboarding |
| Q3 2023 Cohort | 75 companies | 6% YoY | 112% | €52K | "Better retention + expansion; new onboarding working" → Double down |
